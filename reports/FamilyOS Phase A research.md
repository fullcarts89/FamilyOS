# Platforms Commoditize Quotes, Leaving Follow-Through Open

> **Note added after founder review (2026-11-07).** This report is the research record and is left as written. Two readings have been qualified in the decision documents. First, "narrow the wedge" means the *launch* wedge; the north-star product is unchanged (`docs/product/product-contract.md`). Second, the framework bench compared minimal framework examples against a baseline built with the safeguards included, so its results are requirements, not proof that frameworks should be avoided (`docs/research/agent-framework-evaluation.md` §1.3).

As of 2026-11-07, primary sources show that FamilyOS's hero job, "find someone to clean our gutters and help schedule it," is already contested at every individual step. Free or bundled platform agents now handle the research and quote step. Google calls several providers and emails back quotes in about 30 minutes, and Thumbtack, Angi, Yelp and TaskRabbit sit inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Alexa+. Human-backed concierges such as Duckbill ($169/mo for a two-seat household) do the whole job. What no product publicly documents is the combination FamilyOS proposes: several adults approving an agent's vendor actions under explicit roles, durable vendor threads that survive days of back-and-forth, and completion recorded with evidence in a home record the household owns. Confidence that this gap is real is only low to moderate, because it rests on public pages and several close rivals do not document how household sharing works. The demand case is weaker than the PRD assumes. Gutter cleaning is mostly done by owners themselves or skipped (only 21% hire it out). Home-service hiring happens a few times a year at most, AI subscription apps keep subscribers worse than other apps, and family apps that sell at scale earn $79–138 per family per year, well below the PRD's $19–29/mo agent tier. On the technical side, neither LangGraph nor the OpenAI Agents SDK is the durable control plane FamilyOS needs. In a local 32-scenario test with mocked tools, both sent the vendor email twice after a crash, and LangGraph also delivered a tampered email to a substitute recipient after approval. The parts worth reusing are Postgres, a Postgres-native durable library or job queue, an email API, Expo and paid LLM APIs, all at permissive licenses and under about $0.60 per household per month at 1,000 households. The binding constraints are Gmail's restricted-scope regime for reading replies, Yelp's and Google's data terms, US telemarketing law (the TCPA) for AI voice calls, and prompt-injection risk from vendor emails.

## Every leg of the wedge is claimed; only the combination is unclaimed

The PRD frames the main competitive risk as matching Cozi and Ohai feature for feature ([FAMILYOS_PRD.md §13](../FAMILYOS_PRD.md)). The evidence points elsewhere. Family organizers mostly turn documents into calendar entries. Home-record apps are adding AI that turns paperwork into records. The execution layer, meaning products that actually contact vendors, is crowded with marketplaces, platform agents and human concierges. Grades below follow one rule. **Confirmed** means a first-party document, or two or more independent reputable sources, states the capability. **Partially confirmed** means the source is incomplete, ambiguous or secondary only. **Not verified** means no public first-party statement was found, which is not proof the capability is missing. No product was tested hands-on.

### Family organizers stop at capture, except Ohai's medical flow

**Cozi** remains the incumbent. It is Free, then Gold at $39/yr and Max at $79/yr, though its own Max page says $79.99 ([Cozi compare plans](https://www.cozi.com/compare-plans/); [Cozi Max](https://www.cozi.com/cozi-max/)). It has 4.8 stars from 403K iOS ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cozi-family-organizer/id407108860)). One account holds up to 12 people on a shared password. Cozi's FAQ says there is no restricted-access option, so every member can view, edit and delete everything ([Cozi FAQ](https://www.cozi.com/faq/)). Its AI covers only email-to-event import, recipes and meal plans ([Cozi Max post](https://www.cozi.com/blog/why-more-families-are-upgrading-to-cozi-max/)).

**Ohai.ai, Inc.** is the closer analogue. It is the only organizer with first-party evidence of third-party outreach with tracked status, and that outreach covers doctor's appointments only. Ferry Health's human team calls providers, the user chooses from returned options, and a dashboard tracks requested, pending, confirmed and reschedule states ([Ohai doctor scheduling, Aug 1 2026](https://www.ohai.ai/blog/ai-scheduling-assistant-doctor-appointments/)).

Ohai's real prices differ from the PRD's benchmark. Web prices are **$9.99, $19.99 and $29.99 per month**, and App Store prices are $14.99, $24.99 and $39.99 ([How Ohai works](https://www.ohai.ai/how-it-works/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ohai-ai-household-assistant/id6477802468)). The "$9 Family" figure in the PRD traces to getohai.com, a domain the researchers could not load or link to Ohai.ai, Inc.; it is **not verified**. Ohai's base is thin, with 4.4 stars from 228 iOS ratings. A three-week hands-on review found that a recurring automation had to be built by an Ohai employee ([GBRLIFE](https://www.gbrlife.com/blog/ohai-ai-review)). Ohai shows how a vertical can be added through a partner instead of being built, and that cuts both ways for FamilyOS. Ohai could add home services the same way. It has stated no such plan.

The newer AI organizers cluster around document capture. Skylight Sidekick (Plus at $79/yr), Hearth (approval before items appear), FamilyWall (In Tandem, Cozi's sister brand), Sense ($6.99/mo) and Maple all fall here ([Skylight help](https://skylight.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/47278643288091-Email-Events-Recipes-Menus-and-Photos-With-Sidekick); [Hearth](https://www.hearthdisplay.com/pages/features); [FamilyWall](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/familywall-family-organizer/id496889629); [Sense](https://getsense.ai/blog/posts/sense-vs-ohai-2026)).

The clearest market signal is **Ollie**. This text-first family assistant launched June 2, 2026, raised a $7.5M Khosla-led seed, and shut down on **November 6, 2026**. Its CEO cited competition from Muse and others and difficulty raising money ([AI Weekly/TechCrunch](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/ollie-to-shut-down-nov-6-weeks-after-75m-khosla-seed); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/149015-ollie-lands-7-5m-seed-to-build-a-privacy-first-family-ai-assistant/)).

### Home-record apps own the paperwork, not the vendor thread

**HomeZada** is the leader in turning documents into records. Its November 2, 2026 release confirms AI that turns contractor estimates into projects, inspection reports into repair tasks the homeowner chooses, and invoices into maintenance history. Everything is reviewed by the homeowner before it is saved ([HomeZada press](https://www.homezada.com/press/homezada-launches-ai-powered-home-document-management-that-turns-paperwork-into-action)). It went freemium on November 1. Premium costs $118/yr and Deluxe $218/yr. Family members join free, but no permission levels are documented ([HomeZada pricing](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/pricing)).

**HomeTend** on hometend.co (App Store id6797340061) overlaps most with FamilyOS. It offers a shared household, a tradespeople contact book linked to jobs, a "needs booking" state, and receipt-to-vendor extraction, with Plus at $5.99/mo ([hometend.co](https://www.hometend.co/)). It has one iOS rating, and its policies name no legal entity. **Homer**, sold by Home Owner AB, identifies appliances from photos and keeps a searchable service timeline ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/homer-the-home-management-app/id1250049341); [homer.co](https://homer.co/home-timeline)).

Chore apps sell multi-user household use at $20–40 a year. Tody's Premium+ costs $25–80/yr by household size, and Sweepy costs $19.99/yr ([Tody FAQ](https://todyapp.com/faq); [Sweepy App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sweepy-home-cleaning-schedule/id1498897320)). Neither documents skipping a single occurrence of a recurring task. **MyNestLog** is the one record app that claims the FamilyOS loop: it proposes a pro and a time, and you approve. That is an unverified early-access claim on a developer-hosting subdomain with an unnamed operator ([MyNestLog](https://mynestlog-web.onrender.com/)). Hint, co-founded by Martha Stewart, funds pro referrals through affiliate fees, a conflict Fortune questioned ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/29/hint-a-new-ai-startup-co-founded-by-martha-stewart-offers-an-ai-assistant-for-homeowners/)).

These products fall into four execution models. Marketplaces broadcast a request inside their own network: Yelp, Thumbtack, Angi. Google and Gemini call once, with no booking and no durable thread. General agents with approval gates (Muse, Duet, Copilot Tasks, ChatGPT agent) are built around email, serve one user each, and are mostly in preview. Human-backed services (Duckbill, Pine, Honey Homes, Casa) deliver real follow-through for $100–400 a month. Marketplace integrations almost always return the user to the marketplace for the final, liability-bearing hire.

Field data shows why one-shot calling leaves room for a longer-running product. In Invoca's call-analytics data, **26% of Google AI pricing calls went unanswered and 49% of answered calls gave no price**. Home services did worst, because businesses wanted a site visit before quoting ([Invoca](https://www.invoca.com/blog/google-ai-pricing-calls-study)).

History argues against putting humans in the default loop. Fin moved to B2B analytics because human labor was "capital-intensive and hard to scale" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/07/02/sam-lessin-and-andrew-kortina-on-their-voice-assistants-workplace-pivot)). Hello Alfred became a property manager of 50k+ homes ([Propmodo](https://propmodo.com/why-alfred-built-a-property-management-company-to-make-its-technology-better/)). Panasonic's Yohana closed in January 2026 ([MT Newswires](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2591675868)). Homejoy failed on take rate, workers taking clients off-platform, and worker-classification suits ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/248896)).

Ranked by documented capability, Duckbill is the most direct threat. It does the whole job, already sells household plans and plugs into Claude and ChatGPT. Muse and Duet come next. They already have approval gates and run persistently, so adding household sharing is a product decision for them, not a technical leap. Distribution is the larger threat. Alexa+ (free with Prime, whole household), Muse on WhatsApp and Google Search reach families by default. The most defensible remaining wedge is a vendor-neutral home-services project record owned by the household. It would span the multi-day site visit, quote, scheduling and completion cycle, keep evidence attached, and let more than one adult approve actions under defined roles. FamilyOS's proposed limited-caregiver role fills a gap: Cozi explicitly lacks restricted access, and HomeZada sharing reportedly exposes the whole home record ([Zen of Zada](https://zen.homezada.com/2016/02/17/homezada-tip-sharing-your-homezada-account-with-family/)). "Durable follow-up" alone is the weakest leg, since Duckbill, Pine, Duet and Muse each already claim part of it.

## Gutters are a weak hero task in a low-frequency category

The demand evidence supports a real household burden, but not the PRD's chosen first workflow or its price ladder.

**Hiring frequency.** Professional home-service hiring is episodic. Angi's 2024 survey counted 5.6 maintenance, 2.3 improvement and 0.8 emergency projects per household. Its homeowner sample was limited to households that had recently hired help, and "projects" include DIY work, so these counts overstate the general population ([Angi](https://angi.com/press/2024-state-of-home-spending-report)). Harvard's Joint Center found that **20% of owners spent nothing on improvements or repairs** in 2023, and another 15% spent under $500 ([JCHS](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/improving-americas-housing-2025)). Frontdoor's warranty base implies about 1.8 covered repair requests per member-year ([Frontdoor 8-K](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1727263/000119312526073378/ftdr-ex99_1.htm)). Angi's FY2025 10-K counts about 16 million consumer projects, against roughly 86.7 million owner-occupied households, with service requests down 10% and leads down 17% ([Angi 10-K](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)).

**The hero task itself.** The task is worse than average. A LeafFilter survey of 2,000 single-family owners found **52% clean gutters themselves, 21% hire a professional and 36% spend nothing** on upkeep ([LeafFilter](https://leaffilter.com/blog/gutter-protection-report)). LeafFilter sells gutter guards, so treat this as a vendor survey.

A central estimate for a beachhead household is about two to six vendor discoveries a year. That cannot support weekly use. The Bureau of Labor Statistics time-use survey puts household management at about 10 minutes a day per person ([BLS ATUS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.nr0.htm)). Claims about hours saved need care.

**Who carries the load, and who would buy.** Peer-reviewed work shows the felt burden sits in a different domain from home repair. Among about 3,000 US parents, mothers report handling 71% of mental-load tasks and fathers 45%. Home repairs are the father-skewed exception: fathers report 65% and mothers 53% ([Weeks & Ruppanner 2024, JMF](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/12/241212150327.htm)). Mothers earning over $100K report less childcare and housework but **no reduction in mental load** ([Weeks et al., Socius 2025](https://www.bath.ac.uk/announcements/successful-career-women-still-shoulder-the-majority-of-the-mental-load-at-home-new-research)). Outsourcing turns physical work into management work: anticipating, choosing and monitoring. An approval-heavy agent can add to that monitoring unless it truly takes ownership of the task.

Pain also does not reliably become spending. In one Canadian sample only about 2% would spend a $40 windfall on saving time, even though time-saving purchases raise wellbeing ([Whillans et al., PNAS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017PNAS..114.8523W); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/want-to-be-happier-spend-some-money-on-avoiding-household-chores)). The 2% figure comes from a search snippet.

**Willingness to pay and retention.** The price benchmarks are unforgiving. RevenueCat data across 115,000+ apps puts the median monthly subscription at about $8 and the top price at $22.70. Median download-to-paid conversion is 2.8% in North America ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). AI apps earn more per payer but keep fewer subscribers. Annual-plan retention is **21.1% for AI apps against 30.7% for non-AI apps**, and monthly 12-month retention is 6.1% against 9.5% ([PPC Land on RevenueCat](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)).

Family products that sell at scale clear roughly $79–138 per family per year. Skylight tested $99 and settled on $79 because $99 neared "disgust territory" ([RevenueCat Sub Club](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/mark-michael-skylight-sub-club-podcast-2026.md)). Life360 earned $137.63 per paying circle in Q3 2025 and has 3.2M paying circles against 102.4M monthly users ([Life360 Q3 2025](https://investors.life360.com/news-releases/news-release-details/life360-reports-record-q3-2025-results); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9023298/life360-inc-lifx-q2-2026-earnings-call-highlights-surpasses-100-million-mau-milestone-with-38-revenue-growth)).

The PRD's $19–29/mo tier has no family-app precedent. Its real comparables are general AI assistants and Duckbill. Menlo's July 2026 survey of 5,067 US adults found the typical AI payer spends $20–49/mo, 84% of parents use AI, and "almost nothing in consumer AI is priced or designed around the household yet" ([Menlo Ventures 2026](https://menlovc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/menlo_ventures_consumer_ai_report-2026.pdf)). That fits an open wedge, and it equally fits unproven demand.

Rough arithmetic on RevenueCat's AI retention suggests a $25/mo plan earns only about $30–80 per payer over its lifetime unless FamilyOS far outperforms the benchmark.

**Lead fees, memberships and warranties.** Lead-generation revenue will not rescue the model. Angi earns about $44.77 per lead, about $58 per service request (derived), and pros pay whether or not they are hired. Selling and marketing take 49% of Angi's revenue ([Angi 10-K](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)). At two to four hires a year, even Angi's gross fee comes to $115–230 per household. A realistic affiliate share is $20–80, and taking lead money brings in the conflict the FTC sanctioned in its $7.2M HomeAdvisor order ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-order-requires-homeadvisor-pay-72-million-stop-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement)).

Consumers resist paying for access to home services. Angi's combined pro and consumer membership revenue was $5.6M in Q3 2026, about 2% of revenue ([Angi 10-Q](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000105/angi-20260930.htm)). Consumers do pay about $900 a year for home warranties (Frontdoor: 2.11M warranties, 55% gross margin; per-warranty revenue derived). A warranty moves financial risk off the household rather than saving it effort.

**Trust.** Trust data supports approval gating but caps autonomy. In Menlo's survey, 32% of AI users have let AI act without final sign-off. About three-quarters of consumers are uncomfortable with autonomous agent payment ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/consumers-arent-ready-to-delegate-payments-to-ai-agents/)). Only 33% are comfortable letting their AI deal with a company's AI ([IntelligentCIO/YouGov](https://www.intelligentcio.com/north-america/2025/07/24/global-survey-finds-consumers-embrace-personal-ai-assistants-but-demand-human-support-in-key-moments/)).

**Market size and cost to serve.** The market is large in households, so size is not what limits the business. The researchers' own tabulation of the 2024 ACS microdata counts **12.60M owner-occupied, opposite-sex married, dual-employed households with own children**, 7.23M of them earning $150K or more ([Census PUMS](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/data/pums/2024/1-Year/csv_hus.zip); [B25115](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT1Y2024.B25115)). At North American conversion rates, 10% of that segment installing would yield about 35,000 paying households, or roughly $5M ARR at $12/mo.

Cost to serve is small next to price. List-price infrastructure comes to about $64/mo at 10 households, $96 at 100 and $577 at 1,000, or about $0.58 per household. LLM usage is about $0.35 per household per month on Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 list prices ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Frequency, payment and retention limit the economics, not infrastructure.

## Own the control plane; reuse the rest

The PRD asks for a measured spike choosing between LangGraph and the OpenAI Agents SDK ([PRD §9](../FAMILYOS_PRD.md)). That is a false choice. Both are agent-loop libraries. Neither provides timers that last days, an approval service, or idempotent external actions, and both send users to separate durable engines (Temporal, DBOS, Restate, Dapr) for long waits ([Agents SDK running agents](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/running_agents/); [LangGraph checkpointers](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/checkpointers)).

The decision that matters is where durable state lives. The best-supported answer is FamilyOS's own Postgres tables: `work_item`, `approval` holding an exact-payload hash, and `external_action` holding an idempotency key. Durable waits come from either **DBOS Transact**, an MIT library that needs only Postgres, or a Postgres job queue. The agent step then becomes a replaceable, side-effect-free call that returns schema-validated output ([DBOS architecture](https://docs.dbos.dev/architecture)).

This also solves the versioning problem. Replay-based engines must either keep old code running until in-flight runs drain or carry patch branches. For a two-week vendor wait, that means two weeks of old workers. DBOS by default recovers only workflows whose source hash matches the current code ([DBOS upgrading](https://docs.dbos.dev/python/tutorials/upgrading-workflows)). Temporal's pinned workflows need old worker builds kept alive ([Temporal Worker Versioning](https://docs.temporal.io/production-deployment/worker-deployments/worker-versioning)). If the wait is instead a row in `awaiting_external` with a `next_wake_at` time, current code reads it after any deploy, which is the split Restate itself recommends ([Restate versioning](https://docs.restate.dev/services/versioning)).

The dependency trees installed for the benchmark are clean of strong copyleft. LangGraph 1.2.20's tree is MIT, BSD and Apache. The only exceptions are certifi (MPL-2.0) and orjson (MPL-2.0 AND (Apache-2.0 OR MIT)), both weak, file-level copyleft. The OpenAI Agents SDK 0.24.0 tree is likewise MIT, BSD, Apache and ISC, again with certifi under MPL-2.0 ([lg-licenses.csv](../docs/research/evidence/framework-bench/lg-licenses.csv); [oa-licenses.csv](../docs/research/evidence/framework-bench/oa-licenses.csv)).

The rest of the recommended stack is managed services. Supabase Pro ($25/mo) bundles Postgres, Auth, Storage, Realtime and Queues. Row-level security (RLS) is a sound backstop if policies are indexed and the service key never serves user requests, since service keys and views bypass RLS ([Supabase RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security)). Supabase has no household or organization primitive, so FamilyOS builds `household`, `membership` and `invite` tables itself. Clerk is a cost trap: every two-adult household is a billable organization, about $900/mo at 1,000 households ([Clerk pricing](https://clerk.com/pricing)). The email API is Postmark Pro ($16.50 for 10k, with inbound parsing and a `MailboxHash` for routing replies) or Resend ([Postmark inbound](https://postmarkapp.com/developer/user-guide/inbound/parse-an-email)). The client is Expo with free Expo Push. Recurrence uses RFC 5545 RRULE text with skips stored as exceptions, plus a separate interval-after-completion mode, because RRULE cannot anchor recurrence to a completion ([RFC 5545](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545.txt)). Household memory should live in Postgres full-text search plus pgvector under the same RLS, not in mem0, Zep or Letta, whose model-written memories sit outside tenant isolation and weaken citation and deletion.

### Local benchmark: neither framework is safe as the control plane without code around it

*Measured local evidence, not a production benchmark.* On 2026-11-07 an evaluator ran 32 scripted scenarios: 16 on LangGraph 1.2.20 with SqliteSaver, 9 on the OpenAI Agents SDK 0.24.0, and 7 on a minimal control-plane baseline. Tools were mocked, a file-backed fake email provider counted deliveries, and there was no LLM and no network. The OpenAI harness used a deliberately overconfident fake model that always claims success once it sees any tool result. The baseline was written by the evaluator with approvals bound to a payload hash, an outbox, event deduplication and a tenant check, so its passes show design intent, not neutrality. Crashes were injected as a process kill immediately after the provider acknowledged a send ([results.json](../docs/research/evidence/framework-bench/results.json); [harness](../docs/research/evidence/framework-bench/harness/run_all.py)).

| Property tested | LangGraph 1.2.20 | OpenAI Agents SDK 0.24.0 | Control-plane baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restart, then approve | 1 send; waits for vendor reply | 1 send | 1 send |
| Rejection | 0 sends | 0 sends, but the final output says the vendor was emailed | n/a |
| Crash after send, no provider idempotency | **2 sends** (sync and async durability); exit mode: 1 send, but the run rewinds to awaiting approval | **2 sends** when the saved state is re-approved | 1 send; run blocked as delivery unknown, needs a human |
| Crash after send, provider idempotency key | 1 send (provider deduplicated) | not run | 1 send |
| Duplicate approval event | 1 send, but the second "approved" is **consumed as the vendor's reply**; status becomes needs_review | **2 sends** | duplicate ignored; 1 send |
| Payload changed after approval shown | **delivered to attacker@example.test** | 0 sends; failed with a tool-call-ID consistency error (incidental, not designed) | 0 sends; blocked, payload changed since approval |
| Another tenant resumes the run | API takes no identity; anyone with the thread ID can resume | not run | not found |
| Deploy renames the paused step | no error, 0 sends, nothing pending (**run silently dropped**) | loud error for both agent and tool renames; 0 sends | n/a |
| Tool failure | retry policy: 3 attempts, 1 send; retries exhausted then recovered: 1 send | tool always fails, 0 sends, model reports "Done" with exit code 0 | n/a |
| Side effect placed before the approval pause | ran 3 times across 3 invocations | n/a | n/a |
| Reinvoke without an approval | 0 sends; stays at approval | n/a | n/a |
| Saved state at rest | recipient and body in plaintext (28.7 KB) | recipient in plaintext (9.4 KB) | n/a |
| Vendor reply arrives before send | n/a | n/a | rejected as unexpected for current state |

Most of these results confirm documented semantics rather than uncovering bugs. LangGraph documents that a resumed node restarts from its first line and that an unfinished task can run again ([LangGraph interrupts](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langgraph/interrupts)). The OpenAI SDK documents that approval does not guarantee exactly-once execution and advises idempotency keys, server-side state and atomic transitions ([Agents SDK HITL](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/human_in_the_loop/)).

The benchmark makes four requirements concrete:

1. **Approval must be bound to the exact payload and recipient outside the framework.** An approval answer applied to whatever state exists at resume time is how the tampered recipient got the email.
2. **Resume events need typed, single-use identities.** LangGraph's positional resume let an approval answer land where a vendor reply was expected.
3. **Duplicate prevention needs an outbox plus provider idempotency,** and a "delivery unknown" state that stops for a human.
4. **Completion must come from provider and tool records, never from model text.** The "Done" messages after a rejection and after a failed tool came from a deliberately overconfident fake model, so they say nothing about how often real models misreport. They do show the SDK does nothing to stop a misreport.

Two OpenAI SDK cases, rename-on-deploy and tampering, failed loudly. That beats LangGraph's silent drop, but a run waiting weeks still strands. Timing figures (LangGraph 1,415/257 ms, OpenAI 1,551/516 ms, baseline 19/19 ms) measure whole-process wall time including interpreter start and framework import. They are not comparable to each other or to production latency.

## Email reading, vendor data and AI voice carry the hardest constraints

**Gmail.** Gmail's OAuth tiers split the hero workflow in two. Sending as the user needs only `gmail.send`, a *sensitive* scope: roughly 10 business days of verification and no third-party security assessment. Reading vendor replies in the mailbox needs a *restricted* scope; even `gmail.metadata` counts. Restricted scopes bring about six weeks of review, an annual CASA security assessment by a Google-approved assessor, a fit to an approved use case, and Limited Use rules ([Google scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes); [verification FAQ](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817); [User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy)). Limited Use bars humans from reading the data without affirmative consent for that specific data. Workspace policy separately requires granular user confirmation of agent actions ([Workspace developer policy](https://developers.google.com/workspace/workspace-api-user-data-developer-policy)).

The Limited Use rule bears directly on the Gate 1 concierge test. Operators reading Gmail-derived data likely need specific consent, not a blanket clause in the terms. Microsoft Graph needs only `Mail.Send` and free publisher verification ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/publisher-verification-overview)). The cheapest design worth prototyping sends through `gmail.send` with a per-task Reply-To pointing at a FamilyOS inbound address. Replies then arrive without any mailbox-read scope, though some vendors will call or reply to the From address instead.

**Email providers and email law.** Every email provider's acceptable-use policy forbids unsolicited mail. Resend names cold outreach explicitly, and Postmark requires permission-based lists ([Resend AUP](https://resend.com/legal/acceptable-use); [Postmark terms](https://postmarkapp.com/terms-of-service)). A user-approved, one-to-one quote request is arguably a requested service inquiry rather than a list send, but no provider page says so, and FamilyOS should confirm in writing before alpha. Under CAN-SPAM a neutral quote request is likely neither commercial nor transactional. A "Sent with FamilyOS" promotional footer or vendor-recruiting mail would make it commercial, with penalties up to $53,088 per email ([FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).

No general US law requires labelling a human-approved, AI-drafted email. California's BOT Act, Maine §1500-DD and Utah's AI Policy Act all target misleading people about a bot's identity in commerce, so honest identification of the household member and FamilyOS is the safe default ([Maine](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/10/title10sec1500-DD.html); [Perkins Coie on Utah](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/new-utah-ai-laws-change-disclosure-requirements-and-identity-protections-target)).

**Voice and SMS.** AI voice is the riskiest channel. The FCC confirmed that AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA ([FCC 24-17](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)). 47 U.S.C. 227(b)(1)(A) requires prior express consent for such calls to cell phones regardless of purpose ([47 U.S.C. 227](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227)). Many contractors use cell phones as business lines, and no case was found holding that a published number gives consent. SMS requires A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration ([Twilio 10DLC](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc)). Email plus push should carry the MVP, with user-placed click-to-call as the voice fallback.

**Vendor data terms.** Licensing of vendor data is the largest legal risk in the reuse stack. Yelp's API terms, updated 2026-09-22, forbid putting any Yelp content into a generative AI model (§9.4) and storing it beyond 24 hours ([Yelp API terms](https://terms.yelp.com/developers/api_terms/)). Google Maps Platform terms bar storing Places content beyond `place_id` and bar "creating content" or ML use from it. A separate Maps Experience Agent Tools exception allows grounded output that carries source links ([Maps Platform Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms); [Service Specific Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms)). Brave's search API forbids storing results or using them to evaluate AI ([Brave terms](https://api-dashboard.search.brave.com/terms-of-service)). The practical pattern: store only place IDs and URLs, show Places data live with attribution, and build comparisons from facts the vendor states in its replies or on its own site. For licence checks, California's CSLB and Texas's TDLR publish bulk files, which beats scraping lookup pages ([CSLB data portal](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/onlineservices/dataportal/); [TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/LicenseSearch/licfile.asp)).

**Browser agents.** The Ninth Circuit held in *Amazon v. Perplexity* (2026-08-04) that a user-directed agent's access is the user's access for the federal anti-hacking statute (CFAA). It did so on a limited record and expressly preserved terms-of-service claims ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/amazon-v-perplexity-ninth-circuit-opinion.pdf)). DMCA anti-circumvention claims over defeating bot defenses survived dismissal in *Reddit v. SerpApi* ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc)). Browser Use Cloud ships with stealth mode, CAPTCHA solving and residential proxies on by default ([Browser Use stealth](https://browser-use.com/stealth-browsers)). That conflicts with the PRD's instruction to observe website terms. FamilyOS should turn those off and hand logins and CAPTCHAs to a human.

**Children, health, privacy and stores.** Adult-only accounts keep FamilyOS outside COPPA, because information a parent enters about a child is not collected *from* the child. Child logins for chores would bring the app inside the amended rule, whose compliance date was 2026-04-22 ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)). The $25M Alexa settlement shows that deleting data has to be verifiable ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ftc-doj-charge-amazon-violating-childrens-privacy-law-keeping-kids-alexa-voice-recordings-forever)). Medical or caregiver tasks would put FamilyOS under Washington's My Health My Data Act, which has a private right of action ([WA AG](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy)).

Apple guideline 5.1.2(i) requires explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI. Guideline 4.8 requires Sign in with Apple if Google Sign-In is offered. Both app stores require in-app account deletion ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Play deletion](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)). Presenting human work as AI is a fraud risk, not only a marketing risk: DOJ charged Nate's former CEO, and the SEC charged Presto over AI claims that hid human work ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/10/fintech-founder-charged-with-fraud-after-ai-shopping-app-found-to-be-powered-by-humans-in-the-philippines); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/ai-outlook/2025/sec-settlement-highlights-continued-focus-on-ai-washing)). Gate 1's rule against representing manual work as autonomous is therefore load-bearing.

**Contracts and referral licensing.** Under E-SIGN and UETA, a booking accepted through an agent binds the household. UETA §10 lets consumers undo errors when no means of preventing or correcting them was provided, which makes exact-payload approval a legal control as well as a UX feature ([15 U.S.C. 7001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001); [Consumer Reports Innovation Lab](https://innovation.consumerreports.org/?p=10650)). In California, a paid referral service may not solicit or negotiate contracts for contractors ([CSLB Fast Facts](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/resources/industrybulletins/online_marketplace_fast_facts.pdf)). Taking no vendor money is the cleanest MVP posture.

**Prompt-injection security.** FamilyOS combines all three ingredients of what Simon Willison calls the "lethal trifecta": private household data, untrusted vendor content, and the ability to send messages ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)). Adaptive attacks bypassed 12 published prompt-injection defenses with over 90% success ([Nasr et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023)). CaMeL-style separation provably blocks the attacks it models while solving 77% of AgentDojo tasks, against 84% undefended ([CaMeL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813)). Meta's "Rule of Two" says an agent session should hold at most two of three things: untrusted input, sensitive data, and external action ([Meta](https://ai.meta.com/blog/practical-ai-agent-security/)).

That points to three components. A quarantined reader extracts typed fields from vendor replies and has no tools. A privileged planner sees only those extracts. A deterministic sender delivers only the approved payload, to an allowlist of the task's vendors. The frameworks need patching too: the LangChain serialization flaw CVE-2025-68664 and LangGraph's checkpoint flaws enabling remote code execution reinforce treating checkpoints as untrusted, encrypted data ([GHSA-c67j-w6g6-q2cm](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-c67j-w6g6-q2cm); [LangGraph advisories](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph/security/advisories)). The benchmark's finding that checkpoints are stored in plaintext reinforces the same point.

## Eight PRD assumptions collide with the evidence

| PRD assumption | What the evidence says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark "Ohai Family $9/month" ([PRD §12](../FAMILYOS_PRD.md)) | Ohai.ai, Inc. charges $9.99, $19.99 and $29.99 on the web; the $9 figure traces to an unlinked getohai.com | Wrong anchor |
| Main risk is feature parity with Cozi and Ohai (§13) | The real competitors are Google calling, marketplaces in every AI assistant, Duckbill, Muse and Duet | Misdirected |
| Gutters as the hero task (WF-03) | 21% hire it out; 52% do it themselves; repair is the father-skewed, episodic domain | Weak first workflow |
| Email outreach through a connected channel (WF-03 steps 6–7) | Trades work by phone and text; reading replies in Gmail needs a restricted scope plus annual CASA; provider AUPs bar cold mail | Unvalidated and costly |
| $19–29/mo agent tier; $12–25 test (§12, Gate 3) | No family-app precedent above ~$138/yr; AI apps keep 21.1% of annual subscribers | Unsupported |
| Chores plus delegation fixes low frequency (§13) | Chores are free in Cozi, Apple and Google; Angi memberships are ~2% of revenue; no public data supports the bundle | Untested |
| ≥50% invite a second adult (Gate 3) | No public benchmark; inviting is not activation; one partner usually administers | Wrong metric |
| LangGraph vs OpenAI Agents SDK spike (§9) | Neither is the control plane; both sent duplicates in the local test | False choice |

The market assumptions fail in a consistent direction. They overstate how often households need vendor discovery and understate how much of that discovery is now free. The PRD's own hero task is the clearest case: a vendor survey shows most owners do it themselves or skip it, and Google has announced free AI calling for US home-repair businesses (whether gutter cleaning is covered is unverified). Phone and text dominate the channel. Phone is the preferred first contact at 37.2% against 30.5% for email, and text leads for scheduling ([Modernize](https://modernize.com/homeowner-insights/2024-communication-preferences)). Thumbtack's CEO said the platform sees only 30–40% of customer–pro conversation; the rest happens off-platform ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91534514/)). No independent study measures how often contractors answer cold email, so Gate 1 should measure that reply rate directly, by channel, before WF-03's design is locked.

The payer and the sufferer also differ. Fathers skew toward episodic repairs and mothers toward daily anticipation and monitoring, so a home-services-first product risks adoption by the partner who feels less pain ([Weeks & Ruppanner](https://anacweeks.github.io/assets/pdf/Weeks_Ruppanner_2024_JMF.pdf)). The Gate 3 invite target should become a weekly-active rate for the second adult and the share of tasks that adult owns. The pricing test should benchmark against Duckbill ($169/mo for two seats) and general AI subscriptions, not Cozi's $79/yr.

The technical assumptions are closer to right but under-specified. The WF-03 acceptance criterion that an agent restart preserves task state is necessary and not sufficient. In the local test both frameworks preserved state across restarts and still sent duplicate emails. The criterion should add exactly-once delivery or an explicit "delivery unknown" outcome, single-use typed approvals, and approval invalidated when the payload changes. That is a payload-hash check on the PRD's own `Approval.exact_payload` field.

WF-03 step 3, research using approved sources, collides with Yelp's AI ban and Google's content-creation terms unless counsel approves the Places grounding path. WF-02's "skip" and interval schedules need two recurrence modes, because RFC 5545 cannot anchor recurrence to completion. The plan to use Browser Use for bounded workflows needs stealth, CAPTCHA solving and proxies explicitly disabled. The limited-caregiver and child roles in WF-05 are both an opening, since no competitor documents scoped caregiver access, and a legal trigger under COPPA and, for caregiving tasks, Washington's health-data law.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the PRD's emphasis. The agent is the commodity: Google, Meta, Anthropic, Microsoft and Amazon all ship approval-gated agents that reach businesses, and marketplaces plug into all of them. What none of them provides is what FamilyOS's own data model already describes: payload-bound approvals, an idempotent external-action log, `awaiting_external` states that survive deploys, evidence-typed completion, and household roles. The same gap appeared in the local benchmark. The frameworks did not supply exactly-once sends, payload binding or tenant scoping, and the evaluator's baseline had to add them. FamilyOS's defensible product and its hardest engineering are the same thing: a household-owned control plane and record for vendor work. Two strategic consequences follow, both inferences to test. FamilyOS could treat platform agents and marketplaces as channels it orchestrates and records, rather than rivals it must out-research. And it could be judged on whether households trust it as the system of record for who did what, at what price, with what proof, rather than on hours saved.

That reframing changes what Phase A should validate next. Before engineering commits to WF-03, Gate 0 and Gate 1 should test a more frequent or more painful first workflow alongside gutters; childcare coordination and appliance repair are already PRD candidates. They should measure vendor reply rates by channel, price against Duckbill rather than Cozi, and measure whether the second adult actually acts rather than just being invited. Ollie's shutdown after one seed round shows how quickly a standalone family assistant can be squeezed. The evidence leaves room for FamilyOS only as a trust and record layer, not as another assistant.
