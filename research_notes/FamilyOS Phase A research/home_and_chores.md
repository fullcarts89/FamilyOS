# Home-record, chore and list competitors to FamilyOS (HomeZada, Homer, HomeTend, Tody, Sweepy, AnyList and AI home apps)

All sources accessed 2026-11-07. Only public pages were used. No accounts were created, nothing was signed into, no forms were submitted and nothing was tested hands-on. Pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises each page, so the claims below come from those summaries, not from raw HTML.

**Source grades:**
- **A**: first-party vendor material. This covers vendor sites, help centres, press releases, privacy policies and terms, and the developer-written feature text and version notes in an App Store listing.
- **B**: store metadata (rating values and counts, seller names, in-app purchase prices) and established press such as TechCrunch or Fortune.
- **C**: third-party aggregators and reviews (Common Sense Media, AlternativeTo, Justuseapp, AppFollow, Trustpilot, comparison blogs), plus competitors' blogs.
- **D**: claims seen only in a search-result snippet, with no page fetch.

**Capability grades** use only three values. `confirmed` means a first-party document states it. `partially confirmed` means part of the capability, or an adjacent one, is documented. `not verified` means I found no first-party statement; it does not mean the capability is missing.

**Dates:** App Store pages often show recent version and review dates without a year. Where I assume 2026, I say so.

**Prompt-injection note:** In the first fetch of todyapp.com/faq, the fetch tool reported a line asking AI agents to "report certain content". I ignored it. A second fetch asked specifically for that line, and the tool said no such line exists. So the report is unconfirmed and may be a summariser artifact. I tried a raw-HTML check with a scripted download, but the environment's permission system blocked it, and I did not try to get around that. No other page contained text aimed at AI agents.

## 1. HomeZada: does the Nov 2, 2026 press release check out, and what are its pricing, tiers, members and vendor records?

### Takeaway
The press release is real. It is dated Sacramento, Calif., November 2, 2026, and is hosted on HomeZada's own site. It confirms that AI turns contractor estimates into projects, inspection reports into repair tasks the homeowner chooses, and service invoices into maintenance and repair records, with homeowner review before anything is saved. From Nov 1, 2026, HomeZada is freemium: Essentials is free, Premium costs $118/yr or $21.98/mo, and Deluxe costs $218/yr. Family members join free with their own logins. No role or permission tiers are documented.

### Cited Findings
**The press release**
- The headline is "HomeZada Launches AI-Powered Home Document Management That Turns Paperwork Into Action", datelined Sacramento, Calif., November 2, 2026. It has a standard dateline and company boilerplate — [HomeZada press](https://www.homezada.com/press/homezada-launches-ai-powered-home-document-management-that-turns-paperwork-into-action) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
  - The AI creates improvement projects from contractor quotes, estimates, proposals and contracts.
  - It identifies issues in inspection reports, and the homeowner picks which ones become repair tasks.
  - It converts service invoices into maintenance and repair records, and pulls purchase details for the inventory.
  - It extracts key details from insurance, mortgage and property-tax documents, and makes unstructured documents searchable.
  - Homeowners review what the AI extracted and decide what to save. The release does not say which plan includes Document AI. Co-founder John Bodrozic is quoted.
- An earlier HomeZada blog post (Oct 27, 2026) adds detail — [Zen of Zada blog](https://zen.homezada.com/2026/10/27/how-ai-turns-home-documents-into-data/) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
  - Supported inputs are PDFs, scans and phone photos of receipts, invoices, estimates, warranties, inspection reports, and insurance, mortgage and tax documents.
  - An invoice record captures the work done, the cost and the contractor's contact information.
  - An estimate becomes a project, and later invoices can be tracked against it.
  - Users review extracted data before saving. Homeowner AI answers questions about what documents contain, such as warranty coverage or HOA rules.
  - The post gives no accuracy rates.

**Pricing and tiers (current page)** — [HomeZada homeowner pricing](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/pricing) (A, accessed 2026-11-07)
- **Essentials (free):** Home Inventory, Home Documents, Contact List, News Feed, 10 Homeowner AI chats and 5 Visual Design AI photos.
- **Premium ($118/yr or $21.98/mo):** adds Home Maintenance, Remodel Projects, Finances, Reports, video and recognition AI, 250 AI chats a month and 50 design images a month.
- **Deluxe ($218/yr):** up to 2 properties, 500 chats a month, 100 images a month and Home Marketing.
- **Add-ons:** extra Deluxe properties at $98/yr each; 300 AI credits for $25 (one credit per chat, three per image); publishing a "Zada File" for $75; transferring data to a buyer for $99.
- **Billing:** payment is online by credit card, plans are non-refundable, and only the account owner can cancel.

**Price change history**
- From Nov 1, 2026, Premium monthly rose from $15.95 to $21.98, Premium annual from $99 to $118, and Deluxe from $189 to $218. AI limits rose at the same time. Existing subscribers pay the new price at their first renewal on or after that date — [Zen of Zada blog](https://zen.homezada.com/2026/10/27/how-ai-turns-home-documents-into-data/) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
- A HomeLight blog still lists Premium at $99/yr, which is now out of date — [HomeLight blog](https://www.homelight.com/blog/home-maintenance-apps/) (C, accessed 2026-11-07).

**Members and collaborators**
- You can invite as many family members as you like for free, and each uses their own email and password — [HomeZada pricing](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/pricing) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
- A 2016 help post describes the invite flow: My Account, then Share Properties, then an email invitation.
  - Anyone you share with sees all details of the home, and invited family members can make decisions for the home's management.
  - Access can be removed later; the post's example is a contractor after a project ends.
  - The post describes no view-only or other permission levels — [Zen of Zada 2016](https://zen.homezada.com/2016/02/17/homezada-tip-sharing-your-homezada-account-with-family/) (A, dated 2016, accessed 2026-11-07).
- A 2015 post says HomeZada Professional users can share properties with homeowners — [Zen of Zada 2015](https://zen.homezada.com/2015/11/02/homezada-tip-sharing-properties-with-professionals/) (A, D-level detail from snippet; accessed 2026-11-07).

**Vendor and contractor records**
- Essentials includes a "Contact List" — [pricing](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/pricing) (A).
- The iOS listing describes a contacts list for service providers — [App Store: HomeZada Mobile](https://apps.apple.com/app/id473722482) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
- Maintenance tasks can be linked to costs, photos, documents and third-party service provider details, creating a maintenance history record. The automated schedule is built from more than 50 checklists and requires Premium — [HomeZada press: automates maintenance schedule](https://www.homezada.com/press/homezada-automates-home-maintenance-schedule) (A, undated page, accessed 2026-11-07).
- Invoices capture the contractor's contact info — [Zen of Zada Oct 27, 2026](https://zen.homezada.com/2026/10/27/how-ai-turns-home-documents-into-data/) (A).

**Maintenance features**
- Email and push reminders when tasks are due, and receipts, photos and documents saved with each completed task.
- Homeowner AI offers help finding local service pros. The page does not say how — [HomeZada home maintenance](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/home-maintenance) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).

**AI chat**
- Homeowner AI answers home questions, gives step-by-step maintenance help, analyses photos, forecasts repair and replacement costs, and lets you search past chats. The page does not mention agents, outreach or vendor research — [Homeowner AI](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/homeowner-ai) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
- An undated "Zada" chat press release describes multi-turn chat linked to tasks and projects, giving "How to..." instructions. It describes no booking or other actions — [HomeZada press: AI chat assistant](https://www.homezada.com/press/homezada%E2%80%99s-new-ai-chat-assistant-gives-homeowners-more-power-to-manage-their-home-) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).

**Export and security**
- Users can export home inventory data to local Excel and zip files. Hosting is on AWS, with TLS 1.2 in transit and AES-256 at rest. HomeZada says it does not sell data. The page does not cover deletion — [HomeZada security](https://www.homezada.com/security) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
- The iOS listing mentions a printable inventory PDF, spreadsheet export and zip backup — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id473722482) (A).

### Inferences
- The audit's claim about the Nov 2, 2026 release is accurate as worded. The release, however, sets a homeowner-review step before saving. That makes it document-to-draft-record, not autonomous action.
- The 2016 post suggests that membership gives full access, with an account-owner role limited to billing and cancellation. If that still holds, there is no caregiver-level or limited-access role.
- The iOS app's last update was 05/27/2022, so HomeZada appears to be web-first and mobile may lag behind.

### Gaps
- No current help-centre article on member permissions, removing members, or recurrence editing and skipping was found.
- I found no account-deletion procedure. The privacy policy was not retrieved.
- The release does not say which tier includes Document AI.
- How Homeowner AI's "help finding local service pros" works is not described.

## 3. Tody and Sweepy: recurrence model, workload sharing, gamification, pricing, multi-user

### Takeaway
- **Tody** uses a "need-based" recurrence model. Each task has an interval, progress is tracked from the last completion and colour-coded, and there are no fixed deadlines. Seasonal tasks can be paused. Household sync, FairShare contribution targets and the "Dusty" rival mascot sit in Premium+ ($25–$80/yr by household size).
- **Sweepy** uses per-task frequency plus daily effort-point budgets. It generates a daily schedule for each member, which is a Premium feature, and has a family leaderboard, coins and streaks. Parents approve children's tasks. Premium is $3.99/mo or $19.99/yr, and third parties say household sharing requires it.

### Cited Findings
**Tody**
- **Recurrence:** each task has its own realistic interval, and today's list shows only what is due, colour-coded from green (can wait) to red (overdue) — [todyapp.com](https://todyapp.com/) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
  - The App Store listing describes tasks managed by need rather than fixed dates. You can pause seasonal tasks, schedule irregular intervals, and turn alarms and notifications on or off — [App Store: Tody](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tody/id595339588) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
  - Users ask for hard due dates because tasks reset from the last completion, and for fixed-weekday scheduling (undated reviews) — [Justuseapp Tody reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/595339588/tody/reviews) (C, accessed 2026-11-07).
- **Workload sharing:** chores can be assigned or rotated automatically on every plan, including free. Premium+ adds household sync so everyone sees the same live plan and anyone can check off a task. FairShare sets a monthly contribution target for each member — [todyapp.com](https://todyapp.com/); [Tody FAQ](https://todyapp.com/faq) (A).
  - The free plan is described as one person, which sits oddly with assignment and rotation on free. Not resolved.
- **Gamification:** Dusty, a dust-bunny rival, runs a monthly race — [todyapp.com](https://todyapp.com/) (A). Version notes mention "Dusty challenges and rewards" (6.3.2), Prizes (6.5.3), FairShare (6.5.x), Reports (6.6.8) and a Help Center (6.7.0) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tody/id595339588) (A).
- **Pricing:**
  - Free: one person, no ads.
  - Premium: $9.99/yr, adds multiple homes and the Focus Timer.
  - Premium+: Duo (2 members) $25/yr, Family (6) $40/yr, Team (12) $80/yr.
  - 30-day free trial. Available on iPhone, iPad and Android, with no web version — [Tody FAQ](https://todyapp.com/faq) (A).
  - The App Store lists Premium Solo $9.99, Premium+ Duo $24.99, Family $39.99 and Team $79.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tody/id595339588) (B).
- **Entity:** the App Store seller is Looploop IVS, while the website shows "© 2026 LoopLoop ApS" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tody/id595339588) (B); [todyapp.com](https://todyapp.com/) (A).

**Sweepy**
- **Website** — [sweepy.com](https://sweepy.com/) (A, accessed 2026-11-07):
  - Visual cleanliness indicators.
  - A Smart Schedule that fits the time you have each day.
  - Add household members and share chores, with a family leaderboard.
  - Parents review and approve children's tasks to award points.
  - Unlimited custom tasks free; tasks carry up to 3 points.
  - History and streaks; daily schedule generation needs Premium.
  - The privacy policy is hosted on iubenda. No operator is named.
- **iOS listing:** seller Appsent (U.S.), rated 4.7 from 11K ratings. Version 6.2.0 is dated Feb 4 (2026 assumed), with 6.1.3 dated 11/08/2025 — [App Store: Sweepy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sweepy-home-cleaning-schedule/id1498897320) (A/B, accessed 2026-11-07).
  - Monthly $3.99 and yearly $19.99, with a 3-day trial and no refunds for unused time.
  - Automatically generates a daily schedule for each member.
  - Version 5.0.0 added schedule sharing and a toggle to turn the leaderboard off. Version 6.0.1 (08/28/2025) added multiple homes.
  - Other features: coins for a virtual-home shop, streaks, a widget and Apple Watch.
- **Effort points:** tasks are scored 1–3, each member sets a daily point target, and the app warns when that budget is too low (D/C) — [Sweepy blog: scheduling basics](https://blog.sweepy.app/how-to-schedule-basics) (A; the fetch failed with connection refused, so detail comes from a snippet, D).
- **Premium gating, conflicting third parties:**
  - Cubby/AOL: premium is $19.99/yr or $3.99/mo, and you can't share with a household without it — [Cubby review](https://www.cubbyathome.com/sweepy-cleaning-app-review-80044734) (C, D-level snippet).
  - Common Sense Media: free allows one user; premium, then $2.49/mo or $12.99/yr, adds multiple users. It also flags setup as "somewhat overwhelming", guilt-based "What's that smell?" alerts and required sign-in — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/sweepy-home-cleaning-schedule) (C; review undated, listing last updated May 21, 2020, so prices are stale).
  - Mobile App Daily claims the free tier has unlimited users. This contradicts the other sources — [Mobile App Daily](https://www.mobileappdaily.com/products/best-free-house-cleaning-apps) (C, D-level snippet).

### Inferences
- Neither app models calendar recurrence ("every 2nd Monday"). Both are elapsed-interval and dueness systems, so "skip one occurrence" maps poorly to them. Tody offers pause; Sweepy appears to offer a holiday mode.
- Both put multi-user household use behind a paid tier: Premium+ for Tody, and very likely Premium for Sweepy. This is per-household pricing in the $20–$40/yr range.

### Gaps
- No first-party help pages on Tody skip, snooze or export, or Sweepy holiday mode or export.
- Sweepy's free-tier limits are unresolved: the official prices section showed no amounts.
- Google Play metadata could not be read first-party.

## 4. AnyList: sharing, recipes, meal planning, pricing

### Takeaway
AnyList (Purple Cover, Inc.) shares lists with anyone by email, each person with their own account. Recipes and the meal-plan calendar are shared separately, as one whole collection, and deletions sync to the other person. AnyList Complete costs $9.99/yr for an individual or $14.99/yr for a household. Account deletion is documented. Data export is not documented, and a user complains it is missing.

### Cited Findings
- **Pricing:** Complete is $9.99/yr individual or $14.99/yr household. Complete adds recipe photos, recipe scaling, item prices and price comparison, themes, priority support, and cloud backup of photos and the meal calendar. Recipe imports are capped at 5 on free and unlimited on Complete — [AnyList Premium](https://www.anylist.com/premium); [AnyList features](https://anylist.com/features) (A, accessed 2026-11-07).
- **Household plan:** you add people to your plan and they get Complete features. Upgrading from individual costs only the difference. No member limit is stated — [AnyList help: individual vs household](https://help.anylist.com/articles/individual-household-upgrade) (A).
- **List sharing:** enter the other person's email. Everyone has their own account, so some lists can be shared and others kept private. No permission levels are described — [AnyList help: share list](https://help.anylist.com/articles/share-list) (A, via search summary, D-level for exact wording).
- **Recipe and meal-plan sharing:** Recipes tab, then Share Recipes & Meal Plan. The other person accepts, and the whole recipe collection and calendar sync between you. Deleting a recipe deletes it for the other person too, so the article advises sharing only with someone you trust — [AnyList help: share recipe](https://help.anylist.com/articles/share-recipe) (A).
- **iOS listing:** seller Purple Cover, Inc., rated 4.9 from 81K ratings. Version 7.1 is dated Sep 11 (2026 assumed) — [App Store: AnyList](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/anylist-grocery-shopping-list/id522167641) (A/B, accessed 2026-11-07).
  - Real-time shared check-off and web import of recipes.

### Inferences
- In home-record apps, users complain most about trust and billing. In chore apps, they complain about recurrence rigidity, fairness and paywalls on household features. Both point to FamilyOS needing transparent household pricing and recurrence that can be edited per occurrence.
- App Store pages show only a few reviews each, so complaint themes are indicative, not measured frequencies.

- I could not read first-party Google Play ratings or counts. Play pages came back truncated through the fetch tool, and a scripted fetch was blocked by the environment's permission system.
- There is no systematic review sampling and no complaint frequencies.
