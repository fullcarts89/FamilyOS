# PRD v0.1 review: where the evidence is weak

**Date:** 2026-11-07
**Sources:**
- research notes under `research_notes/FamilyOS Phase A research/`
- `docs/research/competitor-matrix.md`
- the local framework bench

Each challenge comes with a proposed revision. "Keep" means the PRD is right.

**Scope of these challenges (added after founder review):** they challenge the PRD's *launch* assumptions: the hero task, pricing, channels and competitors. They do not narrow the north-star product, which stays a shared AI chief of staff covering chores, routines, calendars, coordination, home management, memory and agentic execution. Where a row says "narrow", it means the launch wedge. See `docs/product/product-contract.md`.

## A. Market and product

| # | PRD assumption | Evidence | Verdict and proposed revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | The competitors are Cozi, Ohai, HomeZada and specialist apps | The hero workflow (find, contact, book a pro) is already served by **execution players**. Duckbill: human operators, Household plan $169/mo. Meta Muse: launched 2026-09-08, persistent agent, approval before consequential actions. Claude Duet: approval-gated email with follow-up, US waitlist. Google "check pricing" calls (and Gemini "Call for Me" on Pixel). Alexa+: household, free with Prime. Pine AI. Yelp Assistant's multi-quote requests. Thumbtack's AI intake | **Revise.** For the *execution* part of the product (WF-03), benchmark against execution players. Family organizers remain the benchmark for chores, calendars and coordination (WF-01, 02, 05) and for distribution and habit |
| A2 | The wedge: durable third-party follow-up + evidence-backed completion + shared permissioned agent | Every *single* element is claimed by someone (Duckbill, Pine, Duet and Muse are close on follow-up). What no source documents is the **combination**: several adults approving under roles, vendor threads lasting days, and completion evidence filed into a home record the household owns. Confidence is low to moderate, because it rests on silence | **Narrow the launch wedge, not the product.** For the execution part, the differentiator is the *household-owned, multi-adult, permissioned* layer over vendor coordination, not "follow-up" itself. **Approval before sending is not a differentiator:** Gemini Call for Me, Duet, Muse and Copilot Tasks all gate actions already. The most defensible remaining wedge is a vendor-neutral household project and vendor record for home services (moderate confidence; competitor-matrix summary #10). Test whether families value the multi-adult part (no partner-adoption data was found), and whether they want it inside a unified household system or as a standalone concierge (Phase B and the Gate 0 wedge questions). Muse claims are press-only; there is no first-party Meta page |
| A3 | Gutter cleaning is the hero | One vendor survey (LeafFilter, which sells gutter guards) says 52% of households do it themselves and 21% hire a pro. Home repairs are "episodic", and fathers report more of them (65% vs 53%, Weeks & Ruppanner 2024). The daily load that mothers carry (79% vs 37%) is schedules and childcare | **Revise.** Gutters suit a *demo* but are a weak *retention* driver. Run the concierge on a basket (gutters, appliance repair, handyman, cleaning, lawn) and pick the hero by measured frequency and pain. Frequency can also come from chores and two-adult coordination in the same product, so Phase B tests those alongside delegation. Childcare scheduling is higher-frequency but brings child-data and safety load. Defer it, but record demand for it |
| A4 | Recurring chores plus occasional delegation retain users | No precedent found showing this combination retains. The survivors made frequency structural: Handy auto-enrolled customers in recurring cleaning; home warranties have about 2.1M members at roughly $900/yr | **Unproven; keep as the hypothesis.** Gate 3's "≥ 60% delegate a second task" is the right test. Add a 60-day retention cut |
| A5 | $9–15/mo organization, $19–29/mo agent | RevenueCat 2026: median monthly app price about $8–10, top percentile about $22.70. AI apps retain worse (12-month monthly retention 6.1% vs 9.5%). Family organizers monetize at about $79–138/yr. The human-backed comparable is about $169/mo | **Revise.** $19–29 is at the very top of app pricing *and* below human concierge. In Gate 1, test **per-job pricing** ($15–40 per handled job) against a subscription. Avoid unlimited plans (PRD §12 already says so) |
| A6 | Affiliate or lead fees as revenue (implicit) | Angi earns about $45 per lead. At 2–4 hires a year that is tens of dollars per household, plus the conflict of interest (FTC's $7.2M HomeAdvisor order) and California referral rules | **Don't rely on it.** At most a later, disclosed, counsel-reviewed add-on |
| A7 | Email to vendors works | **No data** on contractor reply rates to email. Weak signals point the other way: about 26% of contractor calls go unanswered, phone and text dominate, and only 30–40% of Thumbtack customer-pro messages stay in its own chat. Pros often won't quote without a site visit | **Largest open risk.** Make the manual hand-off (prefilled text or call script) a first-class P0 path, and in Gate 1 measure reply rate, time to reply and channel per vendor |
| A8 | Ohai = getohai.com + ohai.ai | ohai.ai is Ohai.ai, Inc. (Pembroke, MA). getohai.com was unreachable from the research tooling. Published Ohai prices are $9.99 / $19.99 / $29.99 on the web and $14.99–39.99 in the App Store, not $9/$16 | **Correct the audit.** The founder should check the getohai.com footer manually (about 2 minutes) |
| A9 | Cozi ~399K ratings, Max $79/yr | App Store 4.8 from 403K ratings. Cozi's own Max page and the App Store say $79.99. All AI features are Max-only and work only in the mobile app | Minor correction |
| A10 | Distribution: not addressed | Alexa+, WhatsApp (Muse) and Google Search reach families by default. Ollie (a family AI) shut down on 2026-11-06, weeks after a $7.5M seed, and its CEO cited general agents | **Add a distribution hypothesis** to Gate 0 (e.g. invite-the-partner loop, homeowner communities, realtor or insurer channels). It is a product risk equal to capability |

## B. Technical

| # | PRD assumption | Evidence | Proposed revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Choose LangGraph or the OpenAI Agents SDK after a spike | Neither is a durable engine. The bench showed duplicate sends after a crash (both), a duplicate approval consumed as a vendor reply (LangGraph), an approved payload altered before send (LangGraph), a silent stranding after a deploy (LangGraph) and "Done" claimed after a tool failure (SDK) | **FamilyOS owns the control plane; the MVP does not depend on an agent framework.** LLM calls are stateless (ADR-001). The bench compared idiomatic framework examples against a baseline written with the safeguards built in, so it shows *requirements* (durable state, idempotent sends, payload-bound approvals, tenancy, evidence-based completion), not that frameworks are unusable. A framework can be adopted later inside a step, or more broadly, if it passes the same requirement suite (ADR-001 §3.2) |
| B2 | Browser Use for bounded workflows | Website terms; Browser Use Cloud has stealth and CAPTCHA solving on by default; no independent reliability data; *Amazon v. Perplexity* (9th Cir., 2026-08-04) leaves terms-of-service claims open | **Remove from MVP.** Revisit only for a named site with permission |
| B3 | "Send only through a connected, authorized channel" via email/calendar OAuth | Reading Gmail needs a *restricted* scope: about 6 weeks of verification plus an annual CASA assessment, and Limited Use bans staff reading mail, which breaks a concierge model. `gmail.send` alone is only *sensitive* | **Relay address + `Reply-To` thread tokens.** No mailbox read, ever, in MVP. Optional P1 `gmail.send` |
| B4 | "Research options using approved sources" | Yelp terms bar its content in generative AI. Google Places allows storing `place_id` only and bans creating content from it. Brave bars storing results | **Discovery = the household's own and pasted vendors first; Places rendered live.** Counsel reviews generative use of Places content. Don't compete on discovery, where Google and Yelp are free and better. Compete on coordination and record |
| B5 | Child-visible chores (WF-02) | Child logins bring COPPA (amended rule, compliance 2026-04-22) and the app-store age-signal laws (TX SB 2420 now; CA AB 1043 in 2027). Some LLM API terms bar apps likely used by minors | **Defer child logins.** Children are named members without accounts |
| B6 | Work states `new → … → verified_complete` | The states mix user-facing status with run status and lack `delivery_unknown`, `awaiting_user_decision`, `scheduled` and an honest `done_unverified` | Adopt the split model and evidence levels E0–E4 (ADR §5) |
| B7 | "React Native/Expo or PWA; decide after usability test" | iOS PWA push still needs Add to Home Screen; share sheet and camera are better in native | **Expo now.** The prototype is the usability test and carries forward |
| B8 | Security scanning of uploads (P0) | GuardDuty needs S3; ClamAV needs 3–4 GiB RAM | Keep it P0 for alpha. For the prototype and spike, use a MIME allow-list + re-encoding; add scanning before Phase D |
| B9 | Calendar read integration P1 | Fine. Read-only Google Calendar scopes are sensitive, not restricted | **Keep** |

## C. Process

- **C1. Gate 0 and Gate 1 are founder work, and they matter more than any code here.** Nothing in public evidence establishes willingness to pay or vendor response rates. Recommendation: build only the Phase B prototype until Gate 0 passes, and use it as the interview prop.
- **C2. The concierge test is constrained.** Gmail Limited Use and the FTC precedents on "AI" that was really humans (Nate, Presto) both apply. Run Gate 1 transparently as "FamilyOS team + software" from a FamilyOS address, never from users' mailboxes, and never claim automation that doesn't exist.
- **C3. The GitHub repo `fullcarts89/FamilyOS` is public.** Competitive research, the threat model and the strategy would be world-readable. Make it private before the first push, or explicitly accept that it is public.
